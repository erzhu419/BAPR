\section{Actuator-regime benchmark}
\label{app:benchmark}

The prospective confirmation benchmark keeps HalfCheetah morphology, gravity, reward, observation space, and episode horizon fixed. Only the actuator-coordinate transform changes. Before each physics step, the environment applies
\begin{equation}
    u_t=\clip(\gain_{z_t}a_t+\varepsilon_t,-1,1),
    \qquad \varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.02^2 I).
\end{equation}
For six action dimensions, the four fixed transforms are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{clc}
\toprule
Mode & Affected channels & $\operatorname{diag}(\gain_z)$ \\
\midrule
0 & lower half & $(-1,-1,-1,+1,+1,+1)$ \\
1 & upper half & $(+1,+1,+1,-1,-1,-1)$ \\
2 & even indices & $(-1,+1,-1,+1,-1,+1)$ \\
3 & odd indices & $(+1,-1,+1,-1,+1,-1)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The environment holds the active mode fixed for 250 steps. A switching episode contains four blocks and lasts 1,000 steps. The simulator state is preserved at a switch; the robot is not reset. Each mode therefore defines a persistent stochastic transition distribution rather than a per-step resampling of robot morphology.

\section{Proofs for the action-compensation mechanism}
\label{app:proofs}

The proofs use common transition and reward maps, a frozen canonical policy, and a known family of actuator transforms.

\subsection{Feasibility and pathwise equivalence}

For an invertible $\gain_z$, set $w=\gain_z^{-1}\gain_r q$.
Multiplying $\gain_z\clip(w)=\gain_r q$ by $\gain_z^{-1}$ gives $\clip(w)=w$.
Componentwise clipping fixes exactly the points in $[-1,1]^d$, proving Eq.~\eqref{eq:compensation_feasibility}.
Polarity transforms preserve that box, so every canonical action gives an admissible compensated command.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:equivalence}]
Let $x_t$ denote the complete simulator state and $s_t$ its observation.
Couple reference and compensated rollouts with equal initial states and the same exogenous noise, including any transition or reset randomness.
Assume their complete states agree at step $t$.
The frozen policy then gives the same canonical action $q_t$ in both rollouts, and
\begin{equation}
 \gain_{z_t}a_t^{\mathrm{comp}}
 =\gain_{z_t}\gain_{z_t}\gain_r q_t
 =\gain_r q_t.
\end{equation}
The commands are admissible because sign matrices preserve the action box.
Adding the shared actuator noise and applying the common actuator clipping therefore gives equal executed actions.
The common transition and reward maps give equal next states and rewards.
Induction proves equality for every step, for any polarity-mode sequence.
Returns agree pathwise, so their distributions and integrable expectations agree under the common noise law.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Action-weighted mode error}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:mode_error}]
At the same simulator state, the nominal signal under estimated mode $\hat z_t$ is
$v_t^C=\gain_{z_t}\gain_{\hat z_t}\gain_r q_t$, whereas the reference signal is $v_t^O=\gain_r q_t$.
Their difference is
\begin{equation}
 e_{t,j}=(g_{z_t,j}g_{\hat z_t,j}-1)g_{r,j}q_{t,j}.
\end{equation}
The multiplier is zero when the mode signs agree and has magnitude two otherwise.
Since $g_{r,j}^2=1$, summing the squared coordinate errors proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted_mode_error}.
Componentwise clipping is non-expansive, so adding common noise gives
\begin{equation}
 \|\clip(v_t^C+\varepsilon_t)-\clip(v_t^O+\varepsilon_t)\|_2
 \le \|e_t\|_2.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Causal execution and distinguishability}
\label{app:causal_identification}

Before action $t$, the available information is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_t=\sigma\!\left(s_0,(a_\tau,r_\tau,s_{\tau+1})_{\tau=0}^{t-1}\right).
\end{equation}
The initialization $b_0=1/M$ is fixed.
With measurable frozen maps and fixed tie-breaking, $(b_t,s_t)$ determines $\hat z_t$ and $a_t$.
After execution, the completed transition determines the new predictions, scores and $b_{t+1}$.
Induction therefore makes $a_t$ measurable with respect to $\mathcal H_t$; the current $s_{t+1}$ first affects action $t+1$.

To compare two fixed modes, use the same initial-state law and the same mode-blind causal behavior policy.
If their transformed commands agree on every history reachable under either hypothesis, the common noise law gives the same executed-action distribution.
The common physics, observation and reward maps then give the same next observed transition law.
Induction over the dwell interval makes the two observed trajectory laws identical.
For any binary decision rule $\phi$ under equal priors, their common trajectory law $P$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(\phi\text{ is correct})
 =\tfrac12 P(\phi=z)+\tfrac12 P(\phi=z')\le\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
In the polarity family, a channel on which two modes differ contributes no distinction while its commanded action is zero.
Nonzero commands must also affect observable transitions to permit identification.

\subsection{Finite-horizon return identity and conditional bound}
\label{app:return_proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:return_gap}]
Fix the exogenous mode schedule and horizon $H$.
Let $x_t$ be the complete physical state and define the oracle values by
\begin{align}
 V_H^O(x)&=0,\\
 Q_t^O(x,v)&=\mathbb E\!\left[r_t+V_{t+1}^O(x_{t+1})
   \mid x_t=x,\text{ nominal signal }v\right],\\
 V_t^O(x)&=Q_t^O(x,v_t^O(x)).
\end{align}
These values include actuator noise, any other transition randomness and the evaluation reset convention.
The primary HalfCheetah confirmation has no health terminations.
Controller memory can be added to the state; the oracle ignores this memory, and its physical-state value remains unchanged.
This permits the causal controller to depend on its completed-transition history.

Conditioning on that history and its chosen signal gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E_C\!\left[Q_t^O(x_t,v_t^C)-V_t^O(x_t)\right]
 =\mathbb E_C\!\left[r_t+V_{t+1}^O(x_{t+1})-V_t^O(x_t)\right].
\end{equation}
The stated integrability conditions permit summation and telescoping.
The terminal value is zero, and the common initial-state law gives $\mathbb E[V_0^O(x_0)]=J_O$.
Since $V_t^O(x_t)=Q_t^O(x_t,v_t^O)$, this proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:causal_return_identity}.
Every expectation is taken along the causal controller's trajectory rather than a separately sampled oracle trajectory.

Under the stated local sensitivity condition,
\begin{equation}
 |Q_t^O(x_t,v_t^C)-Q_t^O(x_t,v_t^O)|
 \le L_t(x_t)\|v_t^C-v_t^O\|_2.
\end{equation}
Proposition~\ref{prop:mode_error} gives
\begin{equation}
 \|v_t^C-v_t^O\|_2
 =2\left(\sum_{j:g_{z_t,j}\ne g_{\hat z_t,j}}q_{t,j}^2\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The triangle inequality and the integrable sensitivity budget yield Eq.~\eqref{eq:causal_return_bound}.
An exogenous random schedule can be handled by conditioning on its realization and averaging, provided the same integrability conditions hold jointly.
\end{proof}

The accompanying Lean development formalizes the compensation identities and conditional return arguments.
It is supplied as a standalone package with fourteen environment-independent modules and pinned Lean/Mathlib dependencies.
Its compensation identities concern diagonal polarity transforms; the signed-permutation extension uses the matrix argument following Proposition~\ref{prop:equivalence}.
The recursive Bellman formalization uses sufficient integrability premises over all states and counterfactual signals at finite horizons, stronger than the visited-law conditions used above.
The return bound retains its action-value sensitivity assumption.

\section{Estimator details}
\label{app:estimator}

The frozen inverse model has five independently parameterized heads. Every head is a three-hidden-layer MLP of width 256 with sigmoid linear unit (SiLU) activations and a final $\tanh$. It predicts the nominal pre-noise signal $v_t=\clip(\gain_{z_t}a_t,-1,1)$ rather than the sampled noisy action $u_t$. The transition feature is
\begin{equation}
    x_t=\left[
      \tanh(s_t/5),
      \tanh(s_{t+1}/5),
      \tanh((s_{t+1}-s_t)/0.1)
    \right].
\end{equation}
The released estimator has two training stages. The initialization was trained from scratch for 3,000 updates using robust and true-mode-conditioned controller trajectories from controller seeds 8 and 16. Its training set contained 96,000 transitions: 64,000 stationary and 32,000 switching transitions. The final estimator was warm-started from that model and fine-tuned for 1,500 updates using robust, four fixed-mode specialist, dynamic-oracle, and causal-router trajectories from source seed 4021. This stage contained 82,000 training transitions and 82,000 separate validation transitions. Across both fitting stages, the estimator therefore consumed 178,000 supervised training transitions, excluding the interaction cost used to train the trajectory-producing policies.

Both fitting stages use AdamW with weight decay $10^{-5}$, global gradient-norm clipping at 10, and bootstrap batches of 256 per head. The initialization learning rate is $3\times10^{-4}$ and the final-stage learning rate is $10^{-4}$. True mode is used offline only to construct the nominal-signal target; deployment receives no mode label. The six empirical inverse-residual variances are
\begin{equation}
 (0.1289,0.1030,0.1174,0.1357,0.1063,0.0967).
\end{equation}
Variance is clipped to $[10^{-4},0.25]$ in the model protocol.

The final-stage estimator-training policy seed is 4021 and the validation policy seed is 4049. Training event seeds are 154001 and 154013; validation event seeds are 154101 and 154113. These precede and are disjoint from all pilot and prospective-confirmation policy and evaluation seeds. The prospective protocol does not refit the estimator.

The frozen filter parameters are hazard $0.002$, evidence scale $1.0$, and prior exponent $0.98$. The residual variances include actuator noise and model error and are not estimates of actuator-noise variance alone. The normalized belief used for action $t$ is recorded before incorporating transition $(s_t,a_t,r_t,s_{t+1})$. This convention prevents use of the current next state to choose the current action. The exponent $0.98$ introduces forgetting, so the resulting belief is evaluated as a decision statistic rather than asserted to be a calibrated hidden Markov model posterior.

\section{Prospective training and evaluation protocol}
\label{app:protocol}

\subsection{Policy budgets}

The canonical path has two stages. A source SAC controller trains for 1,400 iterations with 4,000 environment steps and 250 gradient updates per iteration, totaling 5.6M interactions and 350,000 updates. Its full actor, critic, target critic, and temperature state is then fine-tuned in fixed mode 0 for 700 iterations, adding 2.8M interactions and 175,000 updates. Replay and optimizer states are reset at the fork, while these four learned parameter sets are preserved. The final canonical policy therefore receives 8.4M interactions and 525,000 updates. No validation checkpoint is selected; the final policy is evaluated.

SAC, recurrent ESCP, and \resacbaseline{} each receive the same 8.4M interactions and 525,000 updates. This is an equal policy-training budget, not an equal end-to-end data or supervision budget, because BAPR also uses the separately pretrained frozen inverse model and its trajectory-producing policies described in Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}.

The frozen \resacbaseline{} arm uses five critics, a target blend of 75\% independent targets and 25\% ensemble-minimum targets, an LCB coefficient annealed from $-2$ to zero after a 20\% warmup, an actor-anchor coefficient of 0.001, and EMA evaluation with update coefficient 0.005. It sets both \texttt{weight\_reg} and \texttt{beta\_ood} to zero and disables behavior cloning. It is the configured zero-regularization RE-SAC ablation, not the regularized algorithm. The released per-seed training metadata records these settings and final budgets for all three comparison arms. This labeling clarifies the original frozen configuration without changing its return records or decision rule.

The SAC arm uses two critics and hidden width 256. \subsection{Registered decision rule}

The five-seed fresh-policy pilot failed its registered confirmation rule. It was used only to estimate a sample size of ten for the prospective confirmation; no pilot return is pooled with the confirmation. Before prospective training, the algorithm, reference mode, estimator, policy budgets, seeds, event streams, and decision rule were frozen.

For each comparator, the prospective confirmation requires all of the following:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item positive mean paired switching-return difference;
    \item positive lower bound of a two-sided 95\% paired $t$ interval;
    \item at least 8 of 10 positive policy-seed differences;
    \item at least 24 of 30 positive policy-seed--event differences.
\end{enumerate}
The interval is computed from the ten policy-seed mean differences using the $t_{0.975,9}=2.2622$ critical value. Event wins are consistency diagnostics rather than independent samples in the confidence interval.

The mechanism ladder also checks: (i) true-mode compensation has at least 10\% headroom over equal-budget SAC; (ii) causal compensation recovers at least 70\% of the no-compensation-to-oracle gain; and (iii) causal stationary return retains at least 95\% of oracle stationary return. At least eight seeds must pass each check.

\section{Additional result interpretation}
\label{app:additional_results}

\subsection{Why the registered composite decision fails}

BAPR's mean switching advantages over SAC, ESCP, and \resacbaseline{} all have positive lower 95\% confidence bounds. ESCP and \resacbaseline{} also pass the frozen consistency counts. The composite decision has two blockers. First, the SAC comparison has 7/10 seed wins and 23/30 event wins, below 8/10 and 24/30. Second, true-mode compensation exceeds equal-budget SAC by at least 10\% for 7/10 seeds rather than the required 8/10. The mean-effect evidence is positive, while the registered uniformity and headroom requirements are not fully met.

The three non-winning SAC policy seeds are 88021, 88039, and 88179. Their BAPR-minus-SAC switching differences are $-45.1$, $-228.0$, and $-1.2$. True-mode headroom over SAC is only $+2.3\%$, $-2.3\%$, and $+4.1\%$, respectively. Limited canonical-controller headroom is the dominant cause. However, the causal estimator remains below the true-mode return, and this residual loss reverses the small positive true-mode margins at seeds 88021 and 88179.

For clarity, the reported compensation-recovery metric is
\begin{equation}
 R_{\mathrm{comp}}=
 \frac{J_{\mathrm{causal}}-J_{\mathrm{no\text{-}comp}}}
 {J_{\mathrm{oracle}}-J_{\mathrm{no\text{-}comp}}}.
\end{equation}
It measures recovery of the compensation mechanism, not recovery of oracle advantage over SAC. At the aggregate means, $R_{\mathrm{comp}}=95.9\%$, oracle headroom over SAC is 1134.9, inference loss is 124.9, and the final BAPR advantage over SAC is 1009.9.

The median three-step delay is the first post-switch step at which the estimated mode is correct; it does not require the estimate to remain correct thereafter. Averaged over 30 seed--event cells, wrong-route fractions within the first 1, 2, 4, and 8 post-switch steps are 99.8\%, 99.6\%, 82.7\%, and 46.8\%, respectively. The corresponding overall Brier score is 0.0246 and the mean absolute nominal-signal deviation is 0.0104. These diagnostics expose the unavoidable causal transient more directly than aggregate accuracy alone.

\section{Independent information-matched Reacher protocol}
\label{app:reacher_protocol}

\subsection{Task, training and causal information}

The independent confirmation used native Brax Reacher with the generalized backend, its unmodified observation, reward and termination maps, and an explicit 200-step time limit. Only the action coordinates changed: the four gains were $(+1,+1)$, $(-1,+1)$, $(+1,-1)$ and $(-1,-1)$, with reference mode 0. Modes persisted for 50 steps, the simulator state was preserved at switches, and independent Gaussian actuator noise had standard deviation 0.02 before clipping. Stationary evaluation kept one mode for the entire episode.

Four controllers were trained independently per policy seed. Reference SAC trained in mode 0 from scratch; robust SAC and the two conditioned SAC arms trained across persistent modes. Each ran 400 iterations of 4,000 interactions and 250 updates, totaling 1.6M interactions and 100,000 updates. The learner used two critics, hidden width 256, batch size 256, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, and the final checkpoint, with no validation checkpoint selection. Physics sampling and online inverse inference ran on CPU; updates ran on GPU. All training and evaluation used JAX 0.4.38, Brax 0.10.5, Flax 0.10.4 and MuJoCo 3.1.6.

The conditioned actor received the 11-dimensional native observation and four-dimensional pre-action belief. Its 70,916 parameters exceeded the 69,892 parameters of the reference and robust actors by 1,024 input weights. Conditioned SAC directly selected the command, without analytic compensation. In both training and evaluation, transition $(s_t,a_t,r_t,s_{t+1})$ updated only $b_{t+1}$; the action at $t$ used $b_t$. Belief was reset to uniform at episode boundaries. The filtered arms used $(h,c,\delta)=(0.002,1,0.98)$; the instantaneous arms normalized only the latest completed-transition evidence, without the sticky temporal prior. Compensation and conditioned SAC shared the frozen inverse model and belief-update definition within each matched pair, not identical realized histories.

\subsection{Estimator and accounting}

The Reacher inverse model was fitted separately from the HalfCheetah estimator. A disjoint reference-policy provider, seed 94003, received 1.6M interactions and 100,000 updates. Exploration around its compensated commands collected 178,000 labeled training transitions and 82,000 validation transitions, totaling 260,000 calibration interactions. Training and validation event seeds were 205001 and 205101. Five width-256 inverse heads were trained from scratch for 4,500 updates with batch size 256, AdamW learning rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-5}$ and global gradient-norm clipping at 10. The retained estimator seed was 95103, with 704,010 parameters and residual variances $(0.01771,0.03775)$. It was frozen before the independent confirmations and was not refitted on their policies or events.

These historical provider, calibration and fitting costs are charged equally to compensation and belief-conditioned SAC. Memoryless robust SAC has no estimator cost. The comparison therefore matches controller interactions and estimator information between the conditioned routes, not total data against the memoryless control or actor parameter counts across interfaces.

\subsection{Independent seeds, endpoints and controls}

The final matched confirmation used policy seeds
$\{99007,99027,99047,99067,99087\}$, switching-event seeds
$\{209601,209617,209633\}$, and stationary-event base 209701.
There were five paired episodes per switching stream and five per stationary mode. A seed's mean switching return averaged its 15 episodes. The primary criterion was a positive lower 95\% confidence bound for filtered compensation minus filtered conditioned SAC. Win counts were reported without an all-five-win requirement. The instantaneous matched pair was secondary and could not replace a failed primary. The intervals are marginal, without multiple-comparison adjustment.

The full audit retained true-mode/native-reference, uncompensated, all four fixed-compensation, memoryless robust, filtered compensation, instantaneous compensation, fixed inverse head 0, and both conditioned routes. Across five seeds, 13 routes and 15 episodes, all 975 switching episodes retained 200 steps and three observed switches with no termination. Seven routes were also evaluated in all four stationary modes, totaling 700 episodes. True-mode and native-reference returns matched exactly. The best fixed switching route at the group means, mode 3, obtained $-62.913$, compared with $-6.759$ for filtered compensation.

Earlier cohorts remain separate: three-seed mechanism development, an independent five-seed compensation-versus-memoryless comparison, and a three-seed same-information development comparison. \section{Cross-environment evidence boundary}
\label{app:cross_environment}

The primary ten-seed three-comparator confirmation is specific to HalfCheetah actuator-polarity regimes. The independent five-seed Reacher confirmation supports the separate same-information comparison described in Appendix~\ref{app:reacher_protocol}. Other environment screens are retained below.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Ant.} A three-seed true-mode action-compensation audit obtained 4510.7 switching return versus 2588.6 for robust SAC, but only one seed passed the absolute zero-termination criterion. This demonstrates privileged headroom, not a safe causal deployment result.
    \item \textbf{Hopper and Walker2d.} In the tested structured-channel screen, every robust and oracle switching episode terminated before the first scheduled switch. The benchmark therefore lacked a valid adaptation window and cannot support a BAPR comparison.
    \item \textbf{Swimmer.} An independent three-seed screen used all four two-actuator sign transforms, common noise standard deviation 0.02, 250-step dwell intervals, and 8.4M interactions per robust or reference actor. All audited episodes reached 1,000 steps, and true-mode compensation exactly reproduced native reference-mode returns. Nevertheless, its mean return was 50.30 versus 55.04 for robust SAC; only one of three seeds exceeded robust control by both 10\% and five points. The registered oracle-headroom screen failed, so no causal estimator was fitted for this environment.
    \item \textbf{Bus control.} The bus domain contains persistent changes in speed and demand distributions with per-step stochastic passenger arrivals and traffic variation. RE-SAC-derived robustness is effective there, but its modes are not known invertible actuator transforms. Bus evidence is relevant motivation for stochastic non-stationarity, not direct evidence for the action-compensation mechanism.
\end{itemize}

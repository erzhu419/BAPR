\begin{abstract}%
BAPR adapts a frozen policy when the actuator map changes within an episode among known candidates. An inverse model scores candidates from completed transitions, and a causal belief selects an analytic correction to the next action. In MuJoCo, the estimator was fitted only on reference-mode replay with no extra collection. On HalfCheetah this pipeline outperformed SAC sharing the estimator by $2730.2$ (paired 95\% CI $[1662.4,3798.0]$, five seeds), with different policy training distributions. Brax Reacher used separate four-mode calibration and favored compensation for both polarity and signed permutations. With the actor and estimator frozen, soft commands did not clearly improve return over hard selection. In Walker2d, soft commands recovered less often among matched surviving switch windows, and even true-mode compensation frequently fell. HalfCheetah compensation retained an advantage over uncorrected execution at the tested gain mismatches. We derive conditions for signal matching and filter recovery, with core identities formalized in Lean. The earlier Brax HalfCheetah result improved mean return but missed its preregistered composite gate.
\end{abstract}

  non-stationary reinforcement learning, online system identification, policy reparameterization, actuator degradation, Soft Actor-Critic

\section{Introduction}

Robotic and cyber-physical control systems rarely operate under one fixed actuator interface throughout deployment. Motor drive polarity can be inverted after maintenance or firmware updates, servo channels can be cross-wired or remapped across operating modes, and individual actuators can suffer partial power attenuation while remaining stable over an extended dwell interval before switching abruptly. This motivates the piecewise-stationary Markov decision process, in which an unobserved regime indexes the transition kernel and changes within an episode~\citep{Padakandla2020Survey,Lecarpentier2019NonStationary}.

Adaptive reinforcement learning often conditions a policy on recent experience or selects among separately trained policies~\citep{Luo2022ESCP,Rakelly2019PEARL,Zintgraf2020VariBAD,Kumar2021RMA}. This is appropriate when regimes require different physical behavior. When the same desired physical action only needs a different command, another option is to preserve the learned behavior and correct the command. This follows the fault-hiding motivation of virtual actuators~\citep{LunzeSteffen2003VirtualActuator,Seron2011VirtualActuatorBank}.

BAPR separates mode inference from control. It trains one canonical policy in fixed reference coordinates. A transition-only inverse model scores the known candidate transformations from completed transitions, and a sticky belief selects a transformation for the next command. The tested transformations include polarity flips, signed permutations and known diagonal attenuations. The inverse model can use either separate calibration data or the canonical policy's existing reference-mode replay. The policy and estimator are frozen at deployment.

Our specific contribution is to combine transition-only inverse identification with analytic action compensation for hidden, recurring actuator maps. The estimator learns to predict nominal actuation, not a mode label. Since a known candidate map converts a command to a candidate physical signal, an inverse model fitted only on reference-mode data can also score maps absent from calibration, provided it transfers to the evaluation states and commands supply enough excitation. We test reference-only calibration in MuJoCo. The Brax Reacher polarity and signed-permutation studies instead use separate calibration rollouts through all four modes and test compensation with a shared estimator.

We compare compensation with a belief-conditioned policy, using MuJoCo HalfCheetah and Brax Reacher~\citep{Freeman2021Brax} as the main positive cases. Both routes use the same frozen inverse estimator and filter rule on their own trajectories, so their computed beliefs can differ. This comparison evaluates full training and deployment pipelines. A complementary frozen-policy study changes only the belief-to-command rule and measures when posterior averaging helps or hurts. We also test gain mismatch, report failures on legged tasks, and distinguish exact signal-level statements from conditional closed-loop recovery and return bounds.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Non-Stationary and Context-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning}

Non-stationary RL includes continual learning, hidden-parameter MDPs, change-point methods, and meta-RL~\citep{Khetarpal2020ContinualRL,Doshi-Velez2016HiPMDP,Adams2007BOCD,Nagabandi2019MAML}. Context-conditioned methods infer a latent variable from recent experience and pass it to the actor or critic. ESCP learns an environment-sensitive context for sudden changes~\citep{Luo2022ESCP}; PEARL and VariBAD infer task beliefs for meta-RL~\citep{Rakelly2019PEARL,Zintgraf2020VariBAD}. These methods jointly learn representation and control. BAPR instead exploits known actuator-coordinate structure: the inferred regime selects an analytic projection map around a single canonical policy.

Classical Bayesian online changepoint detection maintains a posterior over run lengths and change histories~\citep{Adams2007BOCD}. BAPR instead tracks a fixed-dimensional belief over candidate modes. It tempers the log prior before adding transition evidence.

\subsection{Robust and Ensemble Reinforcement Learning}

Robust MDP methods optimize against transition uncertainty sets~\citep{Iyengar2005RobustDP,Wiesemann2013RobustMDP}, while distributionally robust actor-critic methods extend this idea to function approximation~\citep{Zhou2023NaturalActorCritic,Panaganti2024RobustF}. SAC provides the maximum-entropy off-policy baseline~\citep{Haarnoja2018SAC}. RE-SAC separates aleatoric regularization from epistemic ensemble disagreement and was originally developed for stochastic bus control~\citep{Zhang2026RESAC}. Robust training can produce a useful fallback policy, but a single memoryless compromise policy leaves substantial performance on the table when persistent modes invert or attenuate actuator channels.

\subsection{System Identification and Policy Reparameterization}

Hidden-parameter and contextual RL infer unobserved dynamics variables~\citep{Doshi-Velez2016HiPMDP,Benjamins2021CARL}. UP-OSI predicts dynamics parameters from recent state--action history and passes them to a universal policy~\citep{Yu2017UPOSI}; RMA trains a privileged extrinsics-conditioned base policy followed by a history-driven adaptation module~\citep{Kumar2021RMA}. Known state--action symmetries can also be built into equivariant networks when the active symmetry is known~\citep{VanderPol2020MDPHomomorphic}. BAPR instead treats the active actuator transform as hidden and piecewise-constant, infers it causally via transition-only inverse dynamics without requiring multi-mode policy training, and applies analytic coordinate projection directly in action space.

\subsection{Virtual Actuators and Action Transformation}

Virtual actuators preserve a nominal controller by inserting a reconfiguration block between it and the faulty plant~\citep{LunzeSteffen2003VirtualActuator}. A bank of such blocks can also select among candidate faults using measured residuals~\citep{Seron2011VirtualActuatorBank}. BAPR shares this separation of controller and reconfiguration. It uses a static inverse or projection for known coordinate maps and learns one observation-to-actuation model from reference experience to compare hidden modes. This is a narrower design than general fault hiding and does not give a closed-loop stability guarantee.

Grounded Action Transformation learns how to alter simulator commands so their effects resemble those in a target domain~\citep{Hanna2021GAT}. It also uses inverse dynamics, but learns a grounding map from source and target transitions for transfer. BAPR uses the known candidate maps and infers which one is active within a single deployment episode. Telemetry-Aware Action Rectification also keeps the policy frozen; it learns an action rectifier conditioned on live actuator telemetry~\citep{Sagar2026TeAR}. Our model instead uses completed state transitions without actuator telemetry, and the corrective map is analytic rather than learned.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

We consider a piecewise-stationary MDP with continuous state $s_t\in\mathcal{S}$ and commanded action $a_t\in\mathcal{A}=[-1,1]^d$. A latent regime $z_t\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ with $M\ge 2$ remains fixed over dwell blocks and changes within an episode without resetting the physical simulator state. We distinguish the nominal pre-noise actuator signal
\begin{equation}
    v_t = \clip\!\left(\gain_{z_t}a_t,-1,1\right)
    \label{eq:nominal_signal}
\end{equation}
from the noisy action passed to the physics engine,
\begin{equation}
    u_t = \clip\!\left(\gain_{z_t} a_t + \varepsilon_t,-1,1\right),
    \label{eq:executed_action}
\end{equation}
where $\gain_z\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ is an invertible actuator transform and $\varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$ is zero-mean Gaussian actuator noise ($\sigma=0.02$). The transition and reward are generated from $(s_t,u_t)$; the agent observes $(s_t,a_t,r_t,s_{t+1})$ but never observes $v_t$, $u_t$, or $z_t$.

We study two structural classes of invertible actuator regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Lossless box-preserving symmetries ($\gain_z(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}$):} diagonal polarity matrices $\gain_z=\operatorname{diag}(g_{z,1},\ldots,g_{z,d})$ with $g_{z,i}\in\{-1,+1\}$ (evaluated both on structured $M=4$ subsets and full $M=2^d$ hypercubes up to $M=256$) and non-diagonal orthogonal signed-permutation matrices ($\gain_z^{-1}=\gain_z^\top$).
    \item \textbf{Lossy actuator attenuation ($\gain_z(\mathcal{A})\subsetneq\mathcal{A}$):} diagonal degradation matrices with $0<|g_{z,i}|\le 1$ and $|g_{z,i}|<1$ on weakened motors (e.g., $0.5\times$ torque capacity combined with polarity inversions), where full-amplitude reference commands cannot be physically reproduced within $\mathcal{A}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Belief-Aware Policy Reparameterization (BAPR)}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Canonical Policy, Box-Constrained Projection, and Exact Symmetries}

Let $r\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ denote a fixed reference regime and let $\pi_r:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{A}$ be the deterministic deployment policy trained in that coordinate system. Given target reference actuation $v_t^\star=\gain_r\pi_r(s_t)\in\mathcal{A}$ and regime $z$, BAPR commands
\begin{equation}
    a_t^{\mathrm{comp}}(z)
    = \clip\!\left(\gain_z^{-1}\gain_r\pi_r(s_t),-1,1\right).
    \label{eq:compensation_general}
\end{equation}
Exact pre-noise reproduction satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \gain_z\clip(\gain_z^{-1}v_t^\star,-1,1)=v_t^\star
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \gain_z^{-1}v_t^\star\in\mathcal{A}.
 \label{eq:compensation_feasibility}
\end{equation}
When lossy attenuation ($|g_{z,i}|<1$) violates Eq.~\eqref{eq:compensation_feasibility}, clipping is not merely a heuristic saturation: for any diagonal gain matrix, Eq.~\eqref{eq:compensation_general} is an optimal box-constrained projection under arbitrary nonnegative coordinatewise sensitivity weights (and the unique optimum whenever all weights are strictly positive).

\begin{proposition}[Weighted box-projection optimality and infeasibility residual]
\label{prop:box_projection}
Let $\gain_z=\operatorname{diag}(g_{z,1},\ldots,g_{z,d})$ with $g_{z,i}\ne 0$, let $v^\star\in\mathcal{A}$, and let $w\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^d$ be any nonnegative coordinate weights. Then $a^{\mathrm{proj}}=\clip(\gain_z^{-1}v^\star,-1,1)\in\mathcal{A}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i=1}^d w_i\left(g_{z,i}a_i^{\mathrm{proj}}-v_i^\star\right)^2
    \le \sum_{i=1}^d w_i\left(g_{z,i}a_i-v_i^\star\right)^2
    \qquad \forall\,a\in\mathcal{A},
    \label{eq:weighted_projection}
\end{equation}
and its unweighted Euclidean infeasibility residual equals
\begin{equation}
    \left\|\gain_z a^{\mathrm{proj}}-v^\star\right\|_2^2
    = \sum_{i=1}^d \max\!\left(0,\,|v_i^\star|-|g_{z,i}|\right)^2,
    \label{eq:infeasibility_residual}
\end{equation}
which vanishes whenever $|v_i^\star|\le |g_{z,i}|$ for all $i$ (and identically on $\mathcal{A}$ whenever $|g_{z,i}|\ge 1$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each coordinate $i$, write $g_{z,i}a_i-v_i^\star=g_{z,i}(a_i-v_i^\star/g_{z,i})$. Because scalar clipping onto $[-1,1]$ minimizes $|a_i-x_i|$ over $a_i\in[-1,1]$ with residual $|{\clip(x_i,-1,1)-x_i}|=\max(0,|x_i|-1)$, multiplying by $w_i g_{z,i}^2\ge 0$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted_projection} and $|g_{z,i}\clip(v_i^\star/g_{z,i},-1,1)-v_i^\star|=\max(0,|v_i^\star|-|g_{z,i}|)$. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} and \texttt{LossyProjectionAndFactoredFilter.lean} give the mechanized proof.
\end{proof}

For diagonal polarity transforms, $\gain_z^{-1}=\gain_z$ preserves $\mathcal{A}$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:compensation_general} simplifies without saturation to
\begin{equation}
    a_t^{\mathrm{comp}}
    = \gain_z\gain_r\pi_r(s_t).
    \label{eq:compensation_polarity}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Pathwise equivalence under box-preserving switching]
\label{prop:equivalence}
Let $(z_t)_{t=0}^{H-1}$ be any regime sequence from a box-preserving family ($\gain_z(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}$, including diagonal polarity flips and signed permutations with $\gain_z^{-1}=\gain_z^\top$). Assume all regimes share the same transition and reward maps. Couple a rollout fixed in reference regime $r$ and a rollout following $(z_t)$ via the same initial simulator state and exogenous-noise sequence. If the latter commands Eq.~\eqref{eq:compensation_general} with the true $z_t$ at every step, the two rollouts produce identical executed actions, states, rewards, and finite-horizon returns pathwise, and hence identical return distributions and expectations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At shared state $x_t$, $\gain_z^{-1}\gain_r\pi_r(s_t)\in\mathcal{A}$, so clipping is inactive and $\gain_{z_t}a_t^{\mathrm{comp}}=\gain_r\pi_r(s_t)$. Shared noise and clipping yield identical $u_t$, next state, and reward; induction over $t$ completes the proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Action-weighted consequence of a mode error]
\label{prop:mode_error}
For diagonal polarity transforms, let $q_t=\pi_r(s_t)\in\mathcal{A}$, true mode $z_t$, and estimated mode $\hat z_t$. The pre-noise nominal-signal error relative to reference execution satisfies
\begin{equation}
 e_t=\left(\gain_{z_t}\gain_{\hat z_t}-I\right)\gain_r q_t,
 \qquad
 \|e_t\|_2^2=4\!\sum_{i:\,g_{z_t,i}\ne g_{\hat z_t,i}}q_{t,i}^2,
 \label{eq:weighted_mode_error}
\end{equation}
and componentwise clipping under shared actuator noise is nonexpansive in Euclidean norm.
\end{proposition}
Proposition~\ref{prop:mode_error} shows why unweighted classification accuracy is an incomplete control metric: misclassifying a quiescent channel ($q_{t,i}\approx 0$) causes negligible actuation error, whereas flipping a strongly excited channel induces a $2|q_{t,i}|$ coordinate impulse.

\subsection{Transition-Only Inverse Identification and Self-Calibration}

To identify regimes online without observing $v_t$ or $u_t$, we train an ensemble $\{f_j\}_{j=1}^{K}$ of $K=5$ inverse-dynamics heads (three width-256 SiLU hidden layers with final $\tanh$ output) to predict the pre-noise nominal signal solely from consecutive observations:
\begin{equation}
    \hat v_t^{(j)} = f_j\!\left(
        \tanh(s_t/5),\tanh(s_{t+1}/5),
        \tanh((s_{t+1}-s_t)/0.1)
    \right),
\end{equation}
minimizing mean-squared nominal-signal error
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{inv}}
 =\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}
 \mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t,z_t,s_{t+1})\sim\mathcal{B}_j}
 \left[\frac{1}{d}\|\hat v_t^{(j)}-v_t\|_2^2\right].
 \label{eq:inverse_loss}
\end{equation}
Crucially, $f_j$ receives neither $a_t$ nor $z_t$ as input; $a_t$ enters only after prediction to score candidate regimes via $\tilde v_t(z)=\clip(\gain_z a_t,-1,1)$ and coordinatewise validation residual variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{inv}}^2$:
\begin{equation}
    \ell_t(z)=\log\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}
    \exp\left[-\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}
    \left(
      \frac{(\tilde v_{t,i}(z)-\hat v_{t,i}^{(j)})^2}{\sigma_{\mathrm{inv},i}^2}
      +\log(2\pi\sigma_{\mathrm{inv},i}^2)
    \right)\right].
    \label{eq:emission}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Why transition-only inverse dynamics rather than forward likelihoods or direct classifiers?}
The regimes share a physical transition map given the executed action. This motivates estimating nominal actuation from a completed transition and reusing the estimate across candidates. The inverse ensemble evaluates each transition once, followed by candidate scoring. The shared-physics forward alternative evaluates a candidate-specific state-action input for each mode, which can be batched. We compare both models and report historical direct-classifier and mode-headed forward results separately.

\paragraph{Calibration from reference replay.}
In the MuJoCo v5 suite, SAC trains in fixed reference mode 0 and the estimator uses the policy's own replay with targets $v_t=\clip(\gain_r a_t,-1,1)$. The primary runs use four-step uniform bursts with trigger probability $0.0125$, and a separate control omits the bursts. The estimator requires additional fitting but no additional environment interactions or multimode labels. We evaluate held-out polarity combinations and known diagonal attenuation without refitting. Earlier Brax cohorts relied on separate calibration data and provider policies (Appendices~\ref{app:estimator} and~\ref{app:reacher_protocol}).

The prediction target is expressed in physical action coordinates. For a given $(s_t,u_t)$, the simulator does not depend on which mode and command produced $u_t$. A mode can therefore be new to calibration while its \emph{physical signals} are within the reference data's support. At deployment the learner compares the prediction to $\clip(\gain_z a_t,-1,1)$ for every candidate. This argument does not imply an invertible observation map: different signals may produce similar observations, and wrong commands may visit states poorly represented in reference replay.

\subsection{Tempered Sticky Filtering and \texorpdfstring{$O(d)$}{O(d)} Channel-Factored Inference}

Let $b_t(z)$ denote the causal mode belief available before selecting $a_t$, initialized at $b_0(z)=1/M$. With hazard $h\in[0,1)$, sticky prior $\bar b_t(z)=(1-h)b_t(z)+\frac{h}{M-1}(1-b_t(z))$, evidence scale $c>0$, and prior-forgetting exponent $\delta\in(0,1]$, BAPR updates post-transition beliefs via
\begin{equation}
    b_{t+1}(z)\propto \bar b_t(z)^{\delta}
    \exp\!\left(c\,\ell_t(z)\right),
    \label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
using frozen parameters $(h,c,\delta)=(0.002,1.0,0.98)$ and causal selection $\hat z_t=\arg\max_z b_t(z)$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:bapr}; Appendix~\ref{app:causal_identification}).

\paragraph{Scaling to $M=2^d$ via $O(d)$ channel-factored filtering.}
Joint inference scores $M$ candidates, with $O(Md)$ arithmetic for fixed ensemble size after the inverse prediction. For a polarity hypercube $M=2^d$ ($64$ for HalfCheetah and $256$ for Ant), candidate signals decouple coordinatewise ($\tilde v_{t,i}(\pm 1)=\pm a_{t,i}$). Maintaining $d$ independent binary beliefs $p_{t,i}=\hat{\Pr}(g_{z_t,i}=+1)$ with binary sticky prior $\bar p_{t,i}=(1-h_{\rm ch})p_{t,i}+h_{\rm ch}(1-p_{t,i})$ and per-channel log-mixture score $\ell_{t,i}(\pm 1)=\log\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^K\exp[-(\pm a_{t,i}-\hat v_{t,i}^{(j)})^2/(2\sigma_{\mathrm{inv},i}^2)]$ updates log-odds in $O(d)$ time:
\begin{equation}
    \log\frac{p_{t+1,i}}{1-p_{t+1,i}}
    = \delta\log\frac{\bar p_{t,i}}{1-\bar p_{t,i}}
      + c_{\mathrm{ch}}\left(\ell_{t,i}(+1)-\ell_{t,i}(-1)\right),
    \qquad
    \hat g_{t,i}=2\mathbb{I}[p_{t,i}\ge\tfrac{1}{2}]-1.
    \label{eq:factored_filter}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Channel-factored hypercube exactness]
\label{prop:factored_exact}
For any coordinate-additive channel score $\Phi(g)=\sum_{i=1}^d \phi_i(g_i)$ over $g\in\{-1,+1\}^d$, the $O(d)$ selector $\hat g_i=\arg\max_{s\in\{-1,+1\}}\phi_i(s)$ maximizes $\Phi(g)$ globally across all $2^d$ joint polarity modes, recovering the unique global maximizer whenever each coordinate has a strict margin $\phi_i(g_i^\star)>\phi_i(-g_i^\star)$. This is exact for the product model in Eq.~\eqref{eq:factored_filter}, not for the joint ensemble posterior in Eq.~\eqref{eq:posterior}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{BAPR deployment with joint or channel-factored filtering}
\label{alg:bapr}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Initialize uniform belief ($b_0(z)=1/M$ or $p_{0,i}=1/2$ for $i=1,\ldots,d$) and observe $s_0$
\FOR{$t=0,1,\ldots$}
    \IF{joint filter}
        \STATE $\hat z_t\leftarrow \min\arg\max_z b_t(z)$ and $\widehat{\gain}_t\leftarrow\gain_{\hat z_t}$
    \ELSE
        \STATE $\widehat{\gain}_t\leftarrow\operatorname{diag}(\hat g_{t,1},\ldots,\hat g_{t,d})$ with $\hat g_{t,i}=2\mathbb{I}[p_{t,i}\ge\tfrac12]-1$
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $a_t\leftarrow\clip\!\left(\widehat{\gain}_t^{-1}\gain_r\pi_r(s_t),-1,1\right)$
    \STATE Execute $a_t$, observe $(r_t,s_{t+1})$, and predict $\{\hat v_t^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{K}$ from $(s_t,s_{t+1})$
    \STATE Update $b_{t+1}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:posterior} (joint) or $p_{t+1}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:factored_filter} (factored)
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Closed-Loop Filter Recovery and Generalized Return Decomposition}

Fix horizon $H$ and a mode schedule $z_{0:H-1}$. Write $x_t$ for the simulator state and $s_t$ for its observation. A terminated trajectory stays in an absorbing state and receives zero reward for the remaining steps. Let $v_t^{\mathrm{ref}}=\gain_r\pi_r(s_t)$, $a_t^P=\clip(\gain_{z_t}^{-1}v_t^{\mathrm{ref}},-1,1)$ be the projected true-mode command, and $a_t^C=\clip(\gain_{\hat z_t}^{-1}v_t^{\mathrm{ref}},-1,1)$ be the causal command. The corresponding signals $v_t^P=\gain_{z_t}a_t^P$ and $v_t^C=\gain_{z_t}a_t^C$ enter the simulator before noise and clipping as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:executed_action}.

\begin{theorem}[Two-term return decomposition and sensitivity bound]
\label{thm:return_gap}
Use shared dynamics, reward map, initial-state distribution, and the noise law in Eq.~\eqref{eq:executed_action}. Define $Q_t^P(x,v)$ by applying the fixed signal $v$ with fresh noise and continuing with the projected oracle $P$. Assume the Bellman integrands are measurable and integrable under the step law for the signals used in the comparison, including the counterfactual signal $v_t^P$ at states visited by $C$. Assume the stage rewards and resulting Bellman terms along the compared rollouts are integrable. Then expected return relative to execution in the reference mode decomposes into
\begin{equation}
 J_C - J_{\mathrm{ref}}
 = \underbrace{\left(J_P - J_{\mathrm{ref}}\right)}_{\text{fixed-policy projection contrast}}
 + \underbrace{\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\mathbb{E}_C\!\left[
   Q_t^P(x_t,v_t^C)-Q_t^P(x_t,v_t^P)\right]}_{\text{causal inference gap } (J_C - J_P)},
 \label{eq:causal_return_identity}
\end{equation}
where $J_P=J_{\mathrm{ref}}$ whenever the regime sequence is box-preserving. For polarity regimes ($P=O$), if
$|Q_t^O(x_t,v_t^C)-Q_t^O(x_t,v_t^O)|\leq L_t^{\mathrm{loc}}(x_t)\|v_t^C-v_t^O\|_2$
along states visited by $C$, then
\begin{equation}
 |J_C-J_O|\leq 2\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\mathbb{E}_C\!\left[
 L_t^{\mathrm{loc}}(x_t)\left(\sum_{i:\,g_{z_t,i}\ne g_{\hat z_t,i}}
 \pi_r(s_t)_i^2\right)^{1/2}\right].
 \label{eq:causal_return_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The projection minimizes the instantaneous error in reproducing the reference signal. The contrast $J_P-J_{\mathrm{ref}}$ compares two executions of the \emph{same} actor; it is not a bound on the optimal return under actuator loss. A policy trained for an attenuated actuator might use other channels to achieve the same task.

Equation~\eqref{eq:causal_return_identity} applies the finite-horizon performance-difference identity~\citep{Kakade2002PDL} to the causal policy. If the reward plus oracle continuation is measurable and bounded in absolute value by $B_t(x)$ over all executed actions in the box at state $x$, Gaussian smoothing under independent $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$ actuator noise gives a sensitivity constant $B_t(x)/\sigma$. This is $50 B_t(x)$ at the experimental noise level. Each term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:causal_return_bound} vanishes when the mode estimate is correct. Errors can also occur at initialization and within a dwell. Theorem~\ref{thm:filter_recovery} bounds the probability of correct selection at the end of a specified window under its excitation and error assumptions.

\begin{theorem}[Tempered log-odds cap and finite-window switch recovery]
\label{thm:filter_recovery}
Consider the ideal log-odds dynamics and filter~\eqref{eq:posterior}, with no numerical probability floor:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Zero-hazard log-odds dynamics.} For $h=0$ and positive initial masses, write $s_t$ for the scaled evidence difference between two modes. With $\delta=1$, a dwell of $T_{\mathrm{dwell}}$ steps at $s_t=m>0$ yields $L_0+T_{\mathrm{dwell}}m$. After $N$ opposing steps of $s_t=-m'<0$, the odds favor the new mode exactly when $N>(L_0+T_{\mathrm{dwell}}m)/m'$. For $0<\delta<1$, if $|s_t|\le S$ and $|L_0|\le S/(1-\delta)$, the recurrence $L_{t+1}=\delta L_t+s_t$ keeps $|L_t|\le S/(1-\delta)$.
    \item \textbf{Closed-loop finite-window recovery.} Let $0<h\le (M-1)/M$, $c>0$, $0<\delta\le 1$, and $\sigma_{\mathrm{inv},i}^2>0$ for every channel. Fix an $N\ge 1$ step post-switch window in true mode $z$ starting from any valid causal belief $b_{t_0}$. Assume the candidate signals, predictions and initial belief are measurable under the causal rollout law. For error tolerances $\epsilon_i>0$, define the pairwise excitation margin
    \begin{equation}
        \Gamma_t(z,w;\epsilon)
        = \frac{1}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^d
        \frac{(\tilde v_{t,i}(w)-\tilde v_{t,i}(z))^2
              -2\epsilon_i|\tilde v_{t,i}(w)-\tilde v_{t,i}(z)|}{\sigma_{\mathrm{inv},i}^2}.
        \label{eq:excitation_margin}
    \end{equation}
    Suppose $\min_{0\le\tau<N,\,w\ne z}\Gamma_{t_0+\tau}(z,w;\epsilon)\ge m>0$ holds outside an under-excitation event of probability at most $\alpha$, head prediction errors satisfy $\mathbb{E}[(\hat v_{t_0+\tau,i}^{(j)}-\tilde v_{t_0+\tau,i}(z))^2]\le M_{\tau,j,i}$, and window length $N$ satisfies
    \begin{equation}
        c\,m\sum_{\tau=0}^{N-1}\delta^\tau
        > \delta^N\log\frac{1-h+\frac{h}{M-1}}{\frac{h}{M-1}}.
        \label{eq:recovery_window_condition}
    \end{equation}
    Then $\Pr\!\left(\hat z_{t_0+N}=z\right)\ge 1-\alpha-\sum_{\tau=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^K\sum_{i=1}^d \frac{M_{\tau,j,i}}{\epsilon_i^2}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Under polarity flips, a distinguishing channel contributes positively to the excitation margin when $|a_{t,i}|>\epsilon_i$. Its contribution to the actuation error also decreases as the command magnitude decreases. The bound at the end of the window is independent of prior dwell length through the mixing floor. Its usefulness depends on the excitation and prediction-error terms. Appendix~\ref{app:return_proof} relates the assumptions to the Lean statements.

The deployed code floors probabilities at $10^{-30}$ before taking logarithms. For the positive hazards used by the joint filter, the analytic mixing floor is larger, so this floor is inactive. At $h=0$ it changes the ideal recurrence once a probability is sufficiently small. Consequently the fixed-dwell ablation measures the implemented filter's behavior, not an unbounded dwell-length law.

\subsection{Controller Training Recipes}

The Brax HalfCheetah confirmation and the MuJoCo v5 suite use different reference-policy training recipes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Two-stage Brax confirmation recipe (8.4M steps).} Each prospective Brax-Spring HalfCheetah seed trains a multimode SAC source for 5.6M interactions and fine-tunes the full actor--critic state in fixed reference mode 0 for 2.8M interactions (resetting replay and optimizer states at the boundary), matching the 8.4M online policy interactions and 525,000 updates of SAC, recurrent ESCP, and \resacbaseline{}.
    \item \textbf{Single-stage self-calibrated MuJoCo v5 recipe (500k direct transitions).} Each learner collects 500k physics transitions under \texttt{AutoresetMode.SAME\_STEP}. BAPR trains SAC in fixed reference mode 0, with four-step uniform exploratory bursts at trigger rate $0.0125$ (about $5\%$ of steps; a separate control omits the bursts), then fits a five-head inverse ensemble for 4,500 AdamW updates from its own replay without extra collection. Belief-SAC collects its own 500k multimode transitions and reuses the estimator fitted on the paired BAPR learner's separate 500k reference transitions. Direct collection is matched; total data access is not (Appendix~\ref{app:mujoco_v5_protocol}).
\end{itemize}
In all protocols, BAPR performs zero policy, critic, or estimator updates during deployment.

We first compare reinforcement-learning pipelines with the same causal estimator and then change only the command rule with the actor and estimator frozen. \subsection{Mechanism Diagnostics}

Across the ten confirmation seeds, the causal estimator attains 98.55\% mode accuracy, a Brier score of 0.0246, and a median single-step first-correct switch-detection delay of 3 steps (with all 450 switches detected). Compensation recovery ($R_{\mathrm{comp}}\ge 70\%$) and stationary retention ($\ge 95\%$) both pass on 10/10 seeds. Proposition~\ref{prop:equivalence} is likewise confirmed to machine precision: under coupled simulator states and exogenous noise, true-mode compensation exhibits zero nominal-signal and return discrepancy relative to native reference-mode execution.

For each seed, the net switching advantage over SAC decomposes exactly into
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{causal}}-J_{\mathrm{SAC}}
=\underbrace{(J_{\mathrm{oracle}}-J_{\mathrm{SAC}})}_{\text{controller headroom}}
-\underbrace{(J_{\mathrm{oracle}}-J_{\mathrm{causal}})}_{\text{inference loss}}.
\label{eq:return_decomposition}
\end{equation}
At the ten-seed group means, controller headroom is 1134.9 ($3422.0-2287.1$) and inference loss is 125.0 ($3422.0-3297.1$), yielding the net $+1009.9$ advantage (up to 0.1 display rounding). Theorem~\ref{thm:return_gap} characterizes this 125.0-point gap through oracle action-value differences along the causal trajectory. The three SAC non-wins occur on seeds 88021, 88039, and 88179, where true-mode headroom over SAC is only $+2.3\%$, $-2.3\%$, and $+4.1\%$, respectively. Limited canonical-controller headroom is thus the primary bottleneck, while transient inference loss reverses the two narrow positive margins at seeds 88021 and 88179 (Figure~\ref{fig:v32_diagnostics}).

\paragraph{Temporal filtering helps; evidence for tempering is inconclusive.}
Against instantaneous selection, BAPR gains $1135.6$ return (95\% CI $[752.9,1518.4]$, 10/10 wins) and reduces action-error energy from $881.0$ to $100.6$. At fixed 250-step dwell, setting $h=0,\delta=1.0$ increases median sustained delay from $3.5$ to $19.0$ steps and regret from $63.1$ to $179.5$; BAPR minus this configuration is $+533.1$ ($[359.5,706.8]$, 10/10 wins). This contrast does not test how delay scales with dwell length. At $h=0.002$, BAPR and the ordinary sticky HMM return $3275.2$ and $3279.0$, respectively (paired $-3.7$ $[-22.3,14.9]$, 7/10 wins). At $h=0$, tempering alone is also inconclusive ($+37.5$ $[-100.8,175.9]$). Five heads and one head differ by $-1.6$ ($[-25.7,22.4]$) at positive hazard. Temporal evidence and positive hazard help on this task; the audit does not isolate a return benefit of tempering or ensembling.

\paragraph{Forward-dynamics models and direct mode classifiers vs.\ inverse dynamics.}
We evaluate two controlled identification baselines on Brax HalfCheetah here, supplemented by a strictly structure-matched shared-physics forward model $g_\psi(s_t,\clip(\gain_z a_t,-1,1))$ across all four MuJoCo v5 morphologies in Section~\ref{sec:mujoco_v5}. First, on five held-out HalfCheetah test seeds (\texttt{101}--\texttt{523}) sharing identical calibration/validation splits (\texttt{8}, \texttt{16} / \texttt{24}), replacing transition-only inverse scoring with a mode-headed raw-action forward-dynamics-and-reward ensemble $p_\psi(\Delta s_t,r_t\mid s_t,a_t,z)$ caused sticky-filter mode accuracy to drop from $99.92\%$ (Brier $0.0013$, P90 delay $11.0$ steps) to $53.65\%$ (Brier $0.8389$, P90 delay $369.4$ steps), lowering soft-routed switching return from $2190.5$ to $799.4$ (paired difference $+1391.0$, 95\% CI $[1122.3,1659.8]$, 5/5 wins; MAP routing $+1407.3$, $[876.1,1938.4]$, 5/5 wins). Because that historical baseline predicts mode-specific state--reward heads from raw $a_t$ rather than feeding $\gain_z a_t$ into one shared physics model, Section~\ref{sec:mujoco_v5} additionally tests the structure-matched shared-physics forward ensemble directly on identical Mode-0 replay splits. Second, across three estimator initializations (\texttt{94103}, \texttt{94121}, \texttt{94139}) trained on the exact same 178,000 single-provider transitions and audited on nine held-out canonical policies, replacing $\{f_j\}_{j=1}^5$ with a direct discriminator $p_\phi(z\mid s_t,a_t,s_{t+1})$ collapsed switching return from $2633.0$--$2694.1$ to $-21.3$--$+100.9$ (paired inverse-minus-classifier gains of $+2654.2$, $+2648.3$, and $+2593.2$; 9/9 wins on every initialization).

\paragraph{Scaling to $M=2^6=64$ hypercubes (trade-offs) and lossy $0.5\times$ projection.}
Across 15 four-block episodes sampled from the $M=64$ polarity hypercube (43 distinct modes), channel-factored control achieves $97.89\%$ per-channel accuracy and $2813.0$ return, versus $-198.9$ for no compensation (paired $+3011.9$, 95\% CI $[2622.4,3401.3]$, 10/10 wins). In the original configuration it gives $-369.1$ relative to joint inference ($3182.0$, paired interval $[-578.0,-160.2]$). This is a contrast of configured filters: joint inference uses a mode-change hazard of $0.002$ and evidence multiplier $1.0$, whereas the original product model uses a bit-flip hazard of $0.002$ and multiplier $0.5$. The latter implies probability $1-(1-0.002)^6 \simeq 0.0119$ of at least one flip.

Under $0.5\times$ attenuation the projected oracle has cumulative action-error energy $240.605$, equal to the minimum squared tracking residual along its own trajectory. The fixed-actor projected-minus-unattenuated return contrast is $-978.9$ ($[-1257.2,-700.6]$), and causal minus projected is $-102.5$ ($[-155.9,-49.1]$). The causal route exceeds uncorrected execution by $+1852.0$ ($[1639.2,2064.8]$, 10/10 wins). The tracking residual is unavoidable when reproducing the reference signal; the return contrast does not quantify how well another strategy could perform under attenuation.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What the Results Establish}

The results identify two requirements for reusing the reference controller: the actuator map must preserve enough control authority, and the executed behavior must both be useful for the task and reveal the active map. Neither identification accuracy alone nor access to the true mode guarantees task success.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{What the interface comparison isolates.} The shared-estimator pipelines favor compensation in MuJoCo HalfCheetah and in Reacher's polarity and signed-permutation studies. They differ in policy training distribution and the use of the map in control. With identical actors, posterior averaging has no resolved return gain and yields more unresolved switches than hard selection on Walker2d.
    \item \textbf{What estimation buys.} In MuJoCo, the reference-mode replay sufficed to fit the tested inverse models. Compared with a shared-physics forward ensemble, the inverse ensemble reuses one prediction across candidates; the return contrasts do not establish a general advantage for inverse prediction. Factoring the filter reduces the number of scores, not necessarily the time of a full policy step. Its performance also depends on the evidence scale and hazard.
    \item \textbf{What remains when the mode is known.} The Walker2d true-mode route falls in 64 of 75 paired episodes. Earlier return and detection summaries must therefore be read jointly with survival. The fixed-policy projection analysis distinguishes a signal-level constraint from an end-to-end return limit.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Four limitations bound our conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Known map structure.} Exact compensation covers box-preserving maps, and coordinatewise clipping solves the weighted projection for diagonal gains. The gain-mismatch audit changes only nonzero magnitudes on one task. Sign errors, unknown faults and changes to the dynamics outside the actuator interface are not tested.
    \item \textbf{Fall termination and precision.} Early termination censored $26\%$--$59\%$ of the scheduled switches on Ant, Walker2d and Hopper in the original audit. Detected-switch delays have a different denominator. The wide paired intervals from five policy seeds prevent strong conclusions on these tasks.
    \item \textbf{Training cost and identification support.} Historical Brax training used a separate estimator provider (89.6M interactions for the HalfCheetah cohort; Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}). MuJoCo avoids that cost, but reference replay need not cover the states visited after a bad correction, and indistinguishable transitions remain possible.
    \item \textbf{Conditional guarantees.} The sensitivity and filter theorems assume bounds on action values, excitation or prediction error. The replay diagnostic checks observed excitation and error events; it does not supply a population moment bound or a numerical safety certificate.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

BAPR learns a mode-independent inverse predictor and uses known actuator maps to convert causal estimates into commands. MuJoCo demonstrates that the predictor can be calibrated from reference replay alone; the Reacher studies test compensation with a shared estimator trained on separate multimode data. Frozen-policy diagnostics show why replacing hard selection with a one-step optimal soft command need not improve closed-loop control. In Walker2d, compensation with the true mode still frequently falls.
